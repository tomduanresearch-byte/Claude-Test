## 2026-09-23: Second meeting

**Publisher's notes (verbatim):** "This isnt great. I really dont even like the premise - theres no charm theres no ambition, it feels like a slice of life novel just from the prompt (ure making the world feel constrained and theres no driving force). Fundamentally, cultivation, battle, progression."

**My reading of the notes:** *The Pill Taster* was small and inward: one sect, a murder mystery and a critique of the system. The publisher wants the genre's core pleasures up front: a protagonist with big ambition and charm, a vast and dangerous world with heights worth climbing, a strong driving force, frequent fights, and progression through the realms that the reader can feel. ## 2026-09-23: Third meeting

**Publisher's question (verbatim):** "I do like this. But how can do we maintain the power creep? Like we know hes never gonna die, so does his downside ever really impact the plot?"

**My reading:** Fair point. "Survive and get stronger" only costs something if death is possible, and readers know the protagonist won't die. The Debt has to cost things the reader believes he can really lose, and it has to force choices that change the plot.

**Proposal (a change to the power system, so pitched and not yet applied):**
1. **Heaven collects from the ground, not just the man.** Returning bolts strike everyone and everything inside his circle, and the circle widens with the size of the debt. Where and when he pays becomes a plot decision. Towns, sects and friends have reason to fear him. (This fits the existing Reclaiming rule: Canglan standing in the circle works because everyone in it gets struck.)
2. **Interest, paid in memory.** Unspent banked thunder is repaid at face value. Every bolt he *spends* as Borrowed Thunder is charged interest at collection: Heaven takes a memory, and it takes the ones he values most. The losses are permanent and cannot be won back by growing stronger.
3. **A due date on Heaven's schedule.** This replaces the vague "no stalling forever" rule. Each stroke shortens his grace period. When it runs out, Heaven collects wherever he is standing, ready or not.

**Arc One effects proposed:** Ch. 5: the two blood bolts bring the due date forward into the Climb. Ch. 7: burning sword-thunder to save Dumpling becomes a deliberate, costly choice. Ch. 8: Bai's warning becomes concrete (a debtor's collection burned a city). Ch. 9–10: he refuses to spend blood-thunder on the stair and climbs on pain. Ch. 12: the due date arrives while 3,000 climbers are on the Pillar, so the fight is to clear the stair and get above them. Ch. 13: the collection scorches the Pillar. Ch. 14: some sects back away and Thunderfall bars him, and Stormwatch's rim-facing peak is the one place built to take his payments. Ch. 15: the example cost. Ma tells the story of his name ("have a good address") and he doesn't remember it. The interest on the Ch. 7 sword-thunder took it.

**Power-creep brake:** Higher Heavens mean bigger bolts, wider circles, and fewer places and people who can afford to stand near him. Memories are finite. Stealing more can win any single fight, but only by spending people, places and pieces of himself.

**Question put to the publisher:** approve these three costs (and memory as the interest), or adjust them?
