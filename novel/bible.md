# Series Bible: Thunder-Born

This is the working reference. If something here contradicts a chapter, this file wins until the chapter is revised.

**Rule zero for this world:** everything a character says about the shape of the cosmos is a *belief*, and usually a local, partial or wrong one. The truth lives only in the author-only sections of this file. Nobody in the story, not even Warden Bai, sees the whole map.

## World

### What people believe, tier by tier

**The Dust (mortals, pickers, Thunderfall).**
- "Heaven" is the green land whose edge hangs over the western horizon on clear days. They see its dark rim, its falling rivers (the **Veils**, which turn to mist long before the ground), its shadow on the western Dust, and at night a scatter of lights along its edge.
- Immortals live up there. Some come down the Skygate Pillar; some fly down from the rim to take tribulation on the Anvil.
- Above Heaven there is only sky and the stars, which are "the lamps of the Jade Hall." Folk songs say there are **nine heavens**; the Kiln Street storyteller says **thirty-three** and charges a copper to name them; the mad monk at the broken shrine says the sky has **no top at all**. Nobody really knows, and most don't care.
- The Ruin, three hundred li east, is "the bones of a god who fell," or a mountain range turned upside down by an old war. Almost nobody knows it's part of a broken heaven.
- The Thunder Court is simply the law of Heaven. Wardens are the most frightening people in the world.
- Realms known by name: Qi Condensation, Foundation Establishment, "Golden Core Immortals." Nascent Soul is a word from stories ("Ancestors").

**The Verdant Heaven (Qingtian; its people also say "the Reach").**
- Its sects call their land **Heaven** and themselves "heavenly disciples." The Dust is the floor below.
- They know their land is huge (bigger than the whole Dust under it) and that other lands float above: faint islands and glows they call **the Upper Heavens**. Sects argue about how many; a common Verdant saying is "seven Upper Heavens," and it's wrong.
- They know Cloudsea exists because its disciples sometimes pass through; they consider it a great, distant power.
- They know the Ruin is a **skyfall shard**, a fallen piece of *something*, and they prize its relics. Many assume it fell from one of the Upper Heavens.
- The Thunder Court keeps a **Registry** tower on the Verdant Heaven. Its seat is "above."
- Realms known: up to Nascent Soul in person, Soul Transformation by reputation (a handful of Verdant patriarchs), anything past that as legend.

**Cloudsea and the middle heavens (Mei Canglan's world).**
- Cloudsea is a sky-sea of cloud with sword-islands, far above and to the east of the Verdant Heaven. To Canglan, the Verdant Heaven is "a lower land" and the Dust is where you go to be struck by lightning.
- Canglan knows there are *dozens* of heavens, domains and sky-seas she has heard of and many she hasn't. She considers Cloudsea middling.
- She knows the Ruin's parent was the **Sundered Heaven**, a heaven that broke long ago and scattered its pieces "over a thousand lands." Cloudsea teaches it as history, not as a secret.
- She has heard, once, a teacher say "true" in a way that wasn't about honesty. She doesn't know what it meant.

**The Thunder Court (Warden Bai's view).**
- The Court oversees tribulations across a vast number of lands. Bai is a Warden of the **Southern Registry**, a small office responsible for the Dust under the Verdant Heaven and a few nearby lands. She has never seen the Court's seat and doesn't know exactly where it is.
- Court doctrine: stolen lightning cracks heavens; the Sundered Heaven broke because of theft from Heaven. (Partly false.)
- Bai has read one sealed-record phrase she was not meant to: that the sky has a **ceiling**, and that above it lie **the True Realms**, to which everything called Heaven is "dust on the floor."

### The master map (AUTHOR ONLY; never state in narration)

- **The Boundless Sky.** There is no stack and no count. The sky holds countless lands at every height and scale, from rocks the size of a village to heavens larger than the whole Dust. They drift on slow **sky-tides** (currents of qi) over ages. Height roughly tracks qi density, but not neatly: there are dense low lands, thin high ones, dead zones and storm-belts.
- **Weight.** Each land has a qi **weight**. Living on a land heavier than your realm crushes you: you can visit a land somewhat heavier than you with protection (sect jade, formations) and suffer; much heavier kills you. This is the main brake on power creep and the reason every climb requires a breakthrough.
- **Scale ladder of what Ling Xiao will meet (rough):**
  1. **The Dust** (the ground). One "floor" among many; there are other Dusts under other lands.
  2. **The Verdant Heaven.** A continent-sized land. Weight: Foundation to live, QC 9 to visit. Hundreds of small and mid sects. Arc One ends here.
  3. **The middle heavens.** Cloudsea, the Scarlet Kiln, the Hanging Gardens of Lu, and dozens more. Weights from Core to Nascent Soul. Arcs Two to four.
  4. **The high heavens.** Great domains ruled by ancient sects and clans; weights from Soul Transformation up. Here people finally talk about "the sky" as a whole, and argue about its ceiling. The Thunder Court's true seat, **the Court of Thunder's Measure**, is up here.
  5. **The Ceiling (the Firmament).** A boundary above all heavens, felt more than seen. Tribulation lightning comes *down through it*. Crossing it is what the lower world's legends mean by "ascension," and no one below knows what happens to those who cross.
  6. **The True Realms.** Above the Ceiling. Only a few beings below have seen them. They are not "higher heavens"; they are more *real*: everything below is, in their eyes, a shadow or a floor. Heavenly tribulation is, at root, the True Realms weighing what rises toward them.
- **The Sundered Heaven.** A great heaven of the high tier, called **Yaoxiao** in its own day, which broke about 30,000 years ago. Its fragments fell across a thousand lands and several Dusts. The Ruin east of Thunderfall is one small shard. Relics: **sky-iron** (metal that remembers flying; the heaven-iron spike in Ch. 1 was sky-iron washed out of the Ruin by a flood and carried to the Anvil), **floatstone**, sealed halls, feral spirit-herb gardens, and wall-maps of the sky drawn from high up. Pieces of it still drift loose in the sky and very occasionally fall (**skyfalls**).
- **Why Yaoxiao broke (secret, reveal late):** its sovereign tried to reach through the Ceiling, and a bolt from the True Realms answered. The Court's "theft" doctrine is a cover story. Seed: the Clear-Sky Bolt that made Ling Xiao has the same unregistered taste.

### Economy

- **Spirit stones** (low, mid, high grade) are the currency among cultivators. A low-grade stone is about a month's wages for a Dust laborer.
- **Thunder-hearts**: crystallized lightning left where the final bolt of a tribulation grounds. Used in body-tempering and lightning artifacts. A walnut-sized one fetches about 300 low-grade stones in the Ghost Market. By custom they belong to the candidate's sect.
- **Scorch-picking**: sifting tribulation ground for lightning-glass, fused ore and the remains of failed candidates. Pays better than farming and kills more people.
- **Skyfall relics**: anything from the Ruin. Illegal to trade in the Dust without a Verdant license, so it all goes through the Ghost Market.

- **Stone-Breath Method**: the public mortal method carved on Thunderfall's west wall. In Dust-thin qi, 99 in 100 practitioners stop at QC layer 3.
- **Layers vs realms.** Layer and stage breakthroughs are internal. *Realm* breakthroughs are the big walls; from Core Formation on each one brings a heavenly tribulation.

### Heavenly tribulations

- A tribulation is Heaven weighing what a cultivator has taken from the world against what they can bear. Survive every bolt and the new realm forms flawless. Miss or fail bolts and it forms flawed (a Core Formation cultivator who takes only 8 of 9 has an **eight-bolt core**: weaker, and it will plateau). A flaw **sets** permanently about ninety days after the tribulation.
- Candidates register with the Thunder Court and take tribulation on designated grounds in a Dust (**Anvils**) so their home lands aren't damaged. Thunderfall's Anvil is the largest under the Verdant Heaven. A Warden attends each one to count the bolts.
- **Escalation.** Bolts grow heavier through a tribulation: the first bolts of a Core tribulation weigh about as much as a Foundation realm; the ninth carries the full weight of Core. (This is what makes bolt-eating a heist: *which* bolt he can safely eat depends on where it falls in the sequence.)
- Colours escalate (white, blue, braided, violet). The final bolt is preceded by a silence called **Heaven's breath**, and when it grounds it leaves a thunder-heart.
- A bolt takes on the flavour of the candidate's Dao: a sword cultivator's bolts are cold and cutting, a blood cultivator's are hot and hungry.
- **Interference.** Anyone who steps into another person's tribulation is struck. Normally they are annihilated, and the tribulation grows stronger.
- A sword cultivator meets the final bolt blade-first, and it often forks off the steel toward the nearest metal. A **fork** carries perhaps a tenth of the bolt's force. (Ling Xiao has seen this eleven times. It's his whole plan in Ch. 1.)
- **Clear-Sky Bolt.** A bolt from a cloudless sky with no tribulation behind it. The Court has three on record under the Verdant Heaven in a thousand years. One of them, sixteen years ago, made Ling Xiao.

### Thunder-born (Ling Xiao's body)

Ling Xiao's body was made inside a Clear-Sky Bolt. He doesn't know this in Ch. 1; he learns the word "thunder-born" from Canglan in Ch. 3 (Cloudsea has an old term for it, **Thunder-Womb Body**, which alchemists also know, and that's the danger).

- **Ordinary qi slides off him.** He can pull in a trickle, which is how he reached QC 3 in the thin Dust, and then it stops. Pills barely work on him. Heaven-pressure still tempers his body like anyone's.
- **Rule 1: He eats bolts.** Tribulation lightning that strikes him, if he opens to it, turns into cultivation at once and pushes him as far as his body can hold. Storm lightning has no Dao or weight in it and simply burns him.
- **Rule 2: He keeps their Dao.** Each eaten bolt leaves a **thunder-vein** of its flavour: sword-vein (cutting arcs, cold precision), blood-vein (speed, hunger, harder to control). Veins are power without technique. He must find teachers and fight masters of each Dao to learn to use them.
- **Rule 3: Appetite.** He can digest a bolt up to about one realm heavier than himself. Much heavier kills him. A weakened fragment of a heavier bolt (a fork, a split) can survive in him **undigested as lodged thunder**: a blade in the belly that he can draw on in desperation (it cuts him too) and that slowly wears at him until he can digest it or give it back. Bolts at or below his own weight barely feed him.
- **Rule 4: Theft and return.** A bolt he eats is missing from its owner's tribulation, and they form flawed. He can **channel a bolt back** into its owner once his realm can carry that bolt out through his meridians (for a Core bolt, Foundation). If he does, they complete their realm and he loses that bolt's thunder-vein and any lodged thunder; the cultivation it already built into his body stays.
- **Taste.** He tastes every bolt. Under everything he has ever eaten sits an older taste he has carried since the crater: the Clear-Sky Bolt. He doesn't know what it is until Ch. 15.
- **Why this is a brake:** he can only grow by going where bigger tribulations happen, and every meal makes an enemy.

## Factions

- **Thunder Court (Leiting Si).** Registers and oversees tribulations across countless lands, charges fees, and hunts **bolt-thieves** (its word for anyone who interferes with a tribulation; bolt-*eaters* are a legend the Court insists is a crime). In the Dust its word is law. On the Verdant Heaven it keeps the **Southern Registry**, and sects defer to it. Its true seat is somewhere in the high heavens; Bai has never seen it. Wardens wear plain white and carry a brush and scroll.
- **Cloudsea Sword Sect (Yunhai Jianzong).** A proud orthodox sword sect of Cloudsea, far above the Verdant Heaven. Most Verdant disciples have never met one. Mei Canglan's sect.
- **Skygate Pavilion (Tianmen Ge).** A mid-sized Verdant Heaven sect that administers the Thunderfall Skygate Pillar, runs the twelve-yearly Climb, and taxes the Dust below. Rich and entrenched. Elder Hu's seat.
- **Stormwatch Sect (Wangbao Zong).** A small, poor Verdant sect perched on the rim above the Anvil. It watches tribulations and the upper sky, and is mocked as "cloud-gazers." It owns the **Star-Lens**, an old instrument built from a skyfall relic. Master: Old Yan (Core Formation, flawless nine-bolt core, three hundred years old). Ling Xiao chooses it in Ch. 14.
- **Other Verdant sects recruiting at the Climb:** Verdant Pine Sect (large, orthodox, sword and formation), Ironbark Monastery (body cultivators), Hundred Flavors Hall (spirit cuisine and alchemy; takes Dumpling).
- **Blood Lamp Sect.** A banned demonic sect hiding in the Dust. Its cultivators take tribulations illegally in the Ruin to avoid the Court.
- **Iron Crows.** Thunderfall's picker gang. Control the best rows of the Anvil. Secretly paid by Skygate Pavilion's steward.

## Main characters

- **Ling Xiao (凌霄)**, 16. Scorch-picker, Thunderfall. "The crater baby": found alive in a Clear-Sky Bolt's crater at the Anvil's edge beside his dead parents, strangers nobody in town knew. All that survived of them is a scorched swaddling cloth embroidered with 凌霄 ("above the clouds"). Raised by the Guo family. Cocky, funny, generous, fast-talking and fast-thinking. Reads terrain and people, has watched 41 tribulations from the red stakes and remembers every one. Never picks on anyone smaller. Stuck at QC 3 for two years (once tried eating powdered lightning-glass; does not recommend it). **Wants:** to climb as high as the sky goes, to find out what he is and why he was born in lightning, and to reach what no one in the Dust can picture. **Not** revenge: the bolt is a question, not a target. **Flaw:** he gambles. Every problem looks like a bet worth making. **Signature:** the smile that gets him out of trouble as often as into it.
- **Guo Bao (郭宝), "Dumpling"**, 15. Foster brother and best friend. Built like a rice barrel, QC 2, far stronger than he looks and gentler than anyone expects. Loyal and a worrier. Wants to cook for immortals. Climbs the Skygate and is picked by the Hundred Flavors Hall.
- **Auntie Guo (郭婶, Guo Shen)**, 40s. Dumpling's mother, runs the Guo dumpling shop on Kiln Street. Took the crater baby in "because he was loud and nobody else wanted him." Brisk, practical, unsentimental in speech and not at all in deed. **Uncle Guo** is a Ruin smuggler (Ch. 5).
- **Ling Xiao's parents.** Dead, unnamed in Arc One. See series mysteries.
- **Mei Canglan (梅苍澜)**, 24. Cloudsea Sword Sect prodigy, now Core Formation (early), with an **eight-bolt core** because the fork of her ninth bolt is lodged in Ling Xiao. Cold, exact, proud, honest, with a dry humour that shows up slowly. Her flaw sets in about ninety days, and he can only return the bolt at Foundation. So she has to keep him alive and get him to Foundation fast. Ally and rival, not a prize. Also the reader's first window upward: she casually knows things that make Ling Xiao's world tiny. Flying sword **Frost Reed**, cracked by the eighth bolt.
- **Warden Bai Qiong (白穹)**, appears in her 30s. Thunder Court Warden of the Southern Registry, Nascent Soul (early). Keeps count. Stern, fair, with a bookkeeper's mind and a very dry wit. A big fish in the Dust, a small one in the Court, and she knows it. Hunts Ling Xiao through Arc One, witnesses him give Canglan's bolt back, and rules that she has "no form for a thief who returns the goods." Registers him instead of sealing him. Holds the Court's file on the Clear-Sky Bolt.
- **Elder Hu Zhenyang (胡振阳)**, 183. Skygate Pavilion elder who governs the Thunderfall Skygate. Foundation Establishment (peak), Mountain-Bearing Dao. Stuck for eighty years because he is afraid of his Core tribulation. Lifespan running out. Dignified, patient and very frightened. Learns from Liang that a **Thunder-Womb Body** exists in his Dust, and that alchemical texts say its lightning refined into a pill carries the eater through tribulation. Main antagonist of Arc One. Ends it alive, with a three-bolt core, stripped of office.
- **Hu Qingshan (胡青山)**, 17. Elder Hu's great-grandson, born in the Dust to a branch family, QC 9 at the start. Spear fighter. Disciplined, cold, hard-working, furious that everyone assumes his place was bought. Beats Ling Xiao cleanly on the Skygate (Ch. 10). When he learns what his great-grandfather is doing, he chooses the right side, at a price. Rival who ends Arc One with mutual respect.
- **Big Tan (谭大)**, 20s. Iron Crow enforcer, QC 5. Humiliated in Ch. 1, holds a grudge.
- **Crow-Father Gou Wu (苟五)**, 50s. Head of the Iron Crows, QC 9, iron claw gauntlets. In Skygate Pavilion's pay.
- **Steward Liang Fu (梁福)**, 60s. Elder Hu's steward, Foundation (early), trained alchemist. Recognizes that the thunder-heart Ling Xiao sells has been *fed on*.
- **Daoist Scarlet Lamp (赤灯道人)**. Blood Lamp Sect, Foundation peak going Core; takes an illegal tribulation in the Ruin (Ch. 5). Ling Xiao eats two of his bolts. He survives with a seven-bolt core and a grudge. Recurring threat.
- **Old Yan (燕老)**, about 300. Master of Stormwatch Sect, keeper of the Star-Lens. Has spent his life counting lights above the Verdant Heaven and has stopped at 1,400 because they keep moving. Appears Ch. 14–15.

## Places

- **Thunderfall (Leiluo Cheng).** Mortal city on the edge of the Anvil. Ash, kilns, tea stalls, picker gangs. The Stone-Breath Method is carved on its west wall. The Guo dumpling shop is on Kiln Street. The broken shrine and its mad monk are by the east gate.
- **The Anvil.** Black glass plain, forty li across, fused by millennia of tribulations. Ringed by **four hundred red stakes** (the safe line). **Hollow glass**: bubbles under the crust left by old bolts; it rings half a tone flat when stepped on. The **Scorched Corner**, at the south-east edge by the old picker shacks, is where the Clear-Sky Bolt fell sixteen years ago; nothing is built there now.
- **The Ghost Market.** An underground night market in old lava tubes beneath the Anvil's northern edge.
- **The Ruin.** Three hundred li east. A province-sized shard of the Sundered Heaven, lying upside down: palaces hanging from what used to be the ground, qi-warped beasts, sky-iron seams. In a collapsed hall (Ch. 5) is a **sky-map** carved from high up: hundreds of lands, the Verdant Heaven a thumbnail near the bottom edge, and at the top a carved line with a single bolt drawn coming down through it, labelled with a character no one in Arc One can read (真, "true," in an archaic hand; revealed later).
- **The Skygate Pillar.** A mountain-sized column from Thunderfall's outskirts to the underside of the Verdant Heaven. **9,999 steps** that grow heavier with the Verdant Heaven's weight. A **Challenge Ledge** every thousand steps. Sect scouts watch from the ledges. The top 999 steps (the **Foundation Stair**) demand a Foundation body; historically no Qi Condensation climber has passed step 8,000.
- **The Climb.** Every twelve years. Open to anyone under twenty with a **Skygate token** (100 low-grade spirit stones, or a sponsor). The highest climber chooses their sect first.
- **The Verdant Heaven.** From above: forests, rivers running off its edges as the Veils, hundreds of sects. Bigger than the whole Dust under it; Thunderfall sits under one corner of its shadow.

## Glossary

- **qi**: vital energy. **dantian**: energy centre below the navel. **meridians**: qi channels.
- **the Dust**: the mortal ground. **weight**: a land's qi density. **Heaven-pressure**: how it feels to stand on a land heavier than you.
- **the Veils**: rivers falling off the Verdant Heaven's rim.
- **tribulation / bolt**: Heaven's lightning at realm breakthroughs. **Heaven's breath**: the pause before the final bolt. **thunder-heart**: crystal left by a final bolt. **fork**: the part of a bolt split off a blade.
- **Clear-Sky Bolt**: lightning from a cloudless sky with no tribulation behind it.
- **thunder-born / Thunder-Womb Body**: Ling Xiao's nature. **thunder-vein**: the Dao a digested bolt leaves in him. **lodged thunder**: an undigested bolt held in him.
- **eight-bolt core** (etc.): a flawed realm. **sets**: a flaw becoming permanent (~90 days).
- **scorch-picker**: someone who sifts tribulation ground. **hollow glass**: see the Anvil. **crater baby**: Ling Xiao's nickname in Thunderfall.
- **the Upper Heavens**: what Verdant sects call the lands they see above them. **skyfall / skyfall relic**: something fallen from above, especially from the Ruin. **sky-iron**: relic metal from the Sundered Heaven.
- **the Sundered Heaven**: the broken heaven (Cloudsea's term). **the Ceiling / the Firmament**: the boundary above all heavens (high-heaven term). **the True Realms**: what lies above it (whispered).
- **Warden**: Thunder Court officer. **bolt-thief**: the Court's word for what Ling Xiao is.
- **spirit stones**: currency. **Skygate token**: entry to the Climb.
- **shixiong / shijie**: senior brother / senior sister. **shidi / shimei**: junior brother / junior sister.
- **zhanglao**: Elder. **daozhang**: address for a Daoist cultivator. **Immortal**: what mortals call any cultivator from above.
- **face**: reputation and standing. **Dao heart**: a cultivator's inner resolve.

## Series mysteries (AUTHOR ONLY; reveal slowly)

- **What is Ling Xiao?** A body made inside a bolt from beyond the Ceiling. The Clear-Sky Bolt was not sent from any heaven the Court oversees.
- **Who were his parents, and why were they in the Dust?** Seeds: strangers, fine cloth under their picker rags, a swaddle embroidered with "above the clouds," and they chose the Anvil's edge to give birth. *Tentative, to pitch to the publisher before committing:* they were fugitives from very high up, hiding their child from something above the Ceiling, and the bolt was that something finding them.
- **Why did the Sundered Heaven break?** The Court says theft. The truth: a bolt from the True Realms. Same taste as the Clear-Sky Bolt.
- **What are the True Realms, and why does tribulation come from them?** Arc One shows one glimpse. Answers stay many arcs away.

## Style notes (for me)

- Third person, close on Ling Xiao. His voice carries the narration: quick, wry, confident, with real fear underneath.
- **Don't over-explain the system or the cosmos.** Show one rule per scene, in action. Cosmology arrives as overheard beliefs, arguments and sudden sights, never as a lecture, and never as the full truth.
- **Keep the sky in view and always bigger than anyone thinks.** In almost every outdoor scene the characters can see *something* above them, and what they see should be a little stranger or larger than what they say about it.
- **Every climb shrinks him.** Whenever he arrives somewhere new, show within the arc that the people there are bigger than him and also don't know what's above *them*.
- Every chapter needs one of: a fight, a breakthrough, or a heist or set piece. No chapter is only talk.
- Use English titles in narration ("Elder Hu", "Warden Bai"). Keep pinyin for flavour terms (qi, dantian, li, jin).
